What CiteCheck looks for

Five independent checks on every reference — plus the evidence to settle each one yourself.

?

Does the cited work exist?

Every reference is looked up in Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, arXiv and Europe PMC. A reference no index has heard of — or a DOI that is well-formed but registered nowhere — is flagged as not found. This is the signature of an invented citation.

⊘

Has it been retracted?

Retraction and correction notices are read from Crossref, OpenAlex and Europe PMC. A retracted source cannot support the claim made about it, and citing one is a finding in its own right.

≡

Does the source say what it's cited for?

The source is retrieved — open-access full text where one exists, the indexed abstract otherwise — and each citing sentence is judged separately. A reference can genuinely support one claim while being oversold for another, and that gap is exactly what gets missed by eye.

⚑

Has the numbering slipped?

When the text credits “Pinto et al. [29]” but entry 29 is by someone else entirely, the reference numbering has drifted — and every citation after it may be wrong too.

⧉

Is anything listed twice?

The same work entered under two different numbers, and citation markers that point at no bibliography entry at all.

◈

Can you see the proof?

Every finding comes with three screenshots: the citing sentence highlighted in the manuscript, the top of the cited source, and the matching passage highlighted in place. Checking a verdict takes seconds, not a trip to the library.

Worth knowing. Most publisher pages are paywalled, so many references are judged on their indexed abstract — the report says so, per reference, wherever that applies. A verdict is a prompt to look, not a ruling: the screenshots exist so you can settle it yourself in seconds.
